# How to Make a Two-Person Micro-Bar Last in Caracas (2026)

*A survival-first operating plan for a very small, owner-run bar — you and one partner, no employees. Companion to `bar-venezuela-research.md`. Compiled 12 July 2026.*

## The one idea this whole plan is built on

The research is blunt about *why* bars die in Venezuela: **market saturation, weak demand, and relentless supplier inflation — not red tape, and not licenses** *(CANARES, Aug 2025; the "bureaucracy kills bars" claim was explicitly refuted in verification)* **[VERIFIED]**. Bars fail because **fixed costs keep running when customers don't show up.**

So the entire strategy is: **make your fixed costs so small that a slow week can't kill you, and make every customer so profitable that a good week compounds.** A two-person, no-employee bar is almost purpose-built for this — *if* you resist the urge to scale, over-build, or over-lease.

Your structural advantages over the "generic bar" in the research report:

| Generic bar's biggest risks | Your micro-bar |
|---|---|
| Payroll ≥ USD 240/mo per worker **[VERIFIED]** | **USD 0 payroll** — you and your partner are the labor |
| *Inamovilidad laboral* makes firing costly/hard **[SOURCED]** | **No employees = no inamovilidad exposure** |
| IGTF collection + biweekly filing as a "special taxpayer" | Stay an **ordinary contributor** → you **cannot and do not charge the 3% IGTF**, and skip the special-taxpayer filing treadmill **[VERIFIED — ordinary contributors are not IGTF perception agents]** |
| Big build-out, big rent, big break-even | Tiny footprint, tiny break-even (see model below) |

You will not out-market the saturation. You win by being **impossible to kill** on a slow month, and **memorable enough** to fill the good nights.

## Part 1 — The Concept: your only weapon against saturation

Another generic "bar with cheap beer" in Las Mercedes is a coin-flip against 60–70% failure. A **tight, differentiated, small-format concept** is what survives, because it (a) commands higher margin, (b) creates a reason to seek you out, and (c) keeps waste near zero.

**The model that fits two people with no staff and low capital: a cocktail-led, reservation-friendly micro-bar / neighborhood speakeasy.**

- Caracas already proves the format works — **6310 Speakeasy** operates by reservation **[SOURCED]**.
- **Cocktail economics are the best in the business:** craft-cocktail gross margin runs **~70–75% per drink**, and cocktail bars avoid the perishable-food waste that sinks restaurants **[SOURCED — Diageo/industry benchmarks, not Venezuela-specific]**.
- Two people can run a 20–35 seat room: **one behind the bar, one on the floor/door/cash.** That is the natural capacity ceiling of a no-employee bar — design *to* it, not past it.

**Concept design rules that keep you alive:**
1. **Small, rotating menu (6–10 signature cocktails + beer + a few spirits).** Fewer SKUs = less trapped inventory, less spoilage, faster mastery, tighter buying.
2. **Cocktail-forward, minimal kitchen.** A full kitchen means gas, a bigger sanitary permit class, perishables, and (eventually) staff. Do **snacks/small bites only**, or none. This is a bar, not a restaurant — keep it that way.
3. **An experience worth a story.** The thing people photograph and tell friends about — a signature drink, the room, a ritual — is your zero-cost marketing engine.
4. **A repeatable "regulars" loop.** In a small market, 40 loyal regulars who come weekly beat 400 strangers who come once.

## Part 2 — Unit economics: the numbers that decide survival

**These figures are anchored to real 2024–2026 Caracas/industry data where cited, but the model itself is [ILLUSTRATIVE] — a framework to replace with your own quotes before you commit a dollar.**

### Revenue side (anchored prices)

Las Mercedes drink prices, **El Diario, Feb 2024** **[SOURCED]**: cocktails **$6–12**, spirits **$6–10**, beer bucket (*tobo*) **$10–15**, bottle service **$70–90**.

Assume a modest, realistic small-bar night:

| Assumption | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Avg spend per guest | **~$16** (≈ 2 drinks) | conservative vs. $6–12/cocktail **[SOURCED]** |
| Guests per open night | **35** | small room, realistic, not a full house **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** |
| Open nights/week | **4** (Wed–Sat) | protects margin, matches 2-person stamina **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** |
| **Monthly revenue** | **≈ $9,000** | 35 × $16 × 4 × 4.3 wks |

### Cost side

- **COGS (pour cost):** golden rule = **inputs ≤ 25% of sale price** **[SOURCED]**. On $9k revenue → **~$2,250** COGS, **~$6,750 gross profit (75%)**.
- **Fixed monthly costs — no salaries** *(illustrative, plug your real quotes)*:

| Line | Est. / month | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rent (small local) | **$500–1,500** | 23 m² Las Mercedes local ~$500 **[SOURCED, RE/MAX/MLS 2025]**; go off-prime to protect this number |
| Electricity + generator fuel | **$300–600** | grid + inverter/generator fuel **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** |
| Municipal tax (patente) on gross | **4.75% of revenue** ≈ **$430** | Chacao alcohol-service rate **[SOURCED]** |
| Music licensing (SACVEN + AVINPRO) | **~$50–?** | monthly, **exact tariff [UNVERIFIED]** — confirm |
| Accountant / gestor | **$100–250** | essential even when tiny **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** |
| Card/POS fees | **~3% of card sales** | **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** |
| Supplies, cleaning, misc, security | **$300–500** | **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** |
| **Total fixed** | **≈ $1,900–3,500** | **no payroll — this is the whole point** |

### What the model says

- **Net profit before owner pay** ≈ $6,750 − ~$2,800 fixed ≈ **~$3,900/month**, i.e. **~$1,950 each** for two owners in a modest month — a genuinely viable micro-income in Caracas, earned by *your own labor*, not extracted from staff. **[ILLUSTRATIVE]**
- **The survival number — break-even:** fixed costs ÷ 75% gross margin ≈ **$2,800 ÷ 0.75 ≈ $3,700 revenue/month** to not lose money.
  - That's **~230 guests/month**, or **~14 guests per night** across 4 nights.
  - **Your single most important metric: can you reliably put ~15 people in the room on a given night?** If yes, you survive almost any month. If you're regularly under that, the concept or location is wrong — and you'll know *early*, cheaply.

This is why "small" is the strategy, not a limitation: **a 50-seat staffed bar might need 40+ covers a night just to break even; you need ~15.** The floor is dramatically lower.

## Part 4 — Legal & tax structure to stay deliberately small

1. **Entity:** with two partners and alcohol liability, a **Compañía Anónima (C.A.)** gives you both **limited liability** — worth the modest extra cost over a firma personal **[SOURCED]**. A firma personal is single-owner and exposes personal assets; avoid it for a two-partner licensed bar.
2. **Stay an "ordinary contributor" — do not get designated a Sujeto Pasivo Especial (SPE) if you can avoid it.** Only SPEs are IGTF perception agents; an **ordinary contributor cannot charge the 3% IGTF** and avoids the biweekly SPE filing regime **[VERIFIED]**. Being small here is a *tax advantage*. Ask your accountant how to stay under the SPE threshold.
3. **Get the patente with alcohol authorization *before* the liquor license** — that ordering is a hard gate **[VERIFIED]**. See the critical path in the research report.
4. **Register cleanly and stay compliant on the cheap stuff** (municipal solvency, sanitary Tipo IV, fire permit, SACVEN/AVINPRO). These are *not* what kills bars, but being caught non-compliant can get you shut down. Pay them; they're small.
5. **Keep bolívar/BCV accounting straight even though you price in USD.** Taxes and the municipal minimum reconcile to the BCV rate **[SOURCED]**. A good accountant is cheaper than a SENIAT/municipal problem.

## Part 5 — Inflation-proofing (2025 inflation ≈ 475% **[VERIFIED]**)

This is the mechanic that grinds bars down. Defenses:

1. **Price in USD, reprice inputs constantly.** Your menu prices in dollars are stable; your *costs* move. Recompute pour cost **monthly** and keep every drink at **≤25% cost** — raise a price or swap a spirit the moment a drink creeps past it. Don't let a favorite cocktail quietly go underwater.
2. **Treat non-perishable inventory as a hedge.** Spirits don't spoil. When a distributor has good pricing (or before an announced adjustment), **buy deeper on core bottles** — you're converting cash that inflates into stock that holds value and margin. Balance against not tying up your whole runway.
3. **Minimize perishables.** Every perishable is a spoilage-and-inflation liability. Citrus, garnishes, and any food should be *just-in-time* and tiny. This is a core reason to stay cocktail/beer-led.
4. **Buy from formal distributors that issue fiscal invoices.** It protects your license and IVA position; **contraband spirits risk your permit and sanitary compliance** **[SOURCED, directional]**. Cheaper off-channel bottles are a trap.
5. **Hold a USD cash reserve, not bolívars.** Keep only enough bolívars for imminent tax/fee payments.

## Part 6 — The two-of-you operating model (and the partnership itself)

The no-employee model is your edge — but **two friends in business is one of the most common ways small bars implode.** Address it *before* opening, in writing.

**Roles & labor:**
- Split the two seats clearly: **one runs the bar/product, one runs the floor/door/money.** Rotate deliberately, not chaotically.
- **4 open nights, not 7.** Two people cannot sustainably work 7 nights *and* do daytime buying, cleaning, admin, and repairs. Burnout is a silent killer. Protect closed days for prep, supply runs, and rest.
- Write down who covers whom when one is sick/traveling — a no-employee bar has **zero redundancy** by default. Have 1–2 trusted on-call people you *could* pay per-night informally (as contractors, not staff) for emergencies.

**Money & governance (put in a written partners' agreement / reflected in the C.A. statutes):**
- **Equity split and how profit is drawn** (fixed monthly draw only *after* fixed costs + runway top-up are covered — never draw into the runway).
- **Decision rules** for spending above $X, adding a partner/investor, changing the concept.
- **What happens if one wants out** — buy-out mechanism, valuation method.
- **A "no borrowing from the till" rule**, and a monthly books review both of you attend.

**Tripwires — agree the kill/pivot conditions in advance, while you're rational:**
- e.g. "If we're below break-even (~$3,700 revenue) for **3 consecutive months** after the ramp, we change the concept or location." "If we burn through half the runway, we stop and reassess." Pre-committing to these stops sunk-cost spirals — the thing that turns a survivable setback into a total loss.

## Part 7 — The runway rule (the single most important survival habit)

- **Open with 3–6 months of fixed costs in USD cash, untouched.** (~$10k–18k in this model.) **[ILLUSTRATIVE but firm principle]**
- Treat it as a locked reserve. It exists so a bad month, a broken compressor, a currency swing, or a slow ramp doesn't end you.
- **Refill it first** out of profits before either owner increases their draw.
- Most bars that "fail" simply **ran out of cash before demand matured.** The runway is how you buy the time to become the neighborhood regular spot.

---

## Part 8 — Resilience: power, security, cash

- **Electricity:** a small **inverter/generator ($3,500–9,500) [SOURCED]** is non-optional — an outage on a Saturday isn't just lost sales, it's warm product and a ruined reputation. Budget fuel monthly. A quiet inverter unit (~60 dB) matters in a small room.
- **Security:** cameras, a lockbox, disciplined cash handling, good relationships on the block. **Specific costs [UNVERIFIED]** — get local quotes. Minimize cash on premises (favor transfers/cards, which also keeps you clear of received-cash-USD IGTF questions).
- **Cash mix:** encourage **bolívar transfer / card** payment. It reduces on-site cash (security) and sidesteps IGTF-on-cash-dollars complexity — and as an ordinary contributor you're not charging IGTF anyway **[VERIFIED]**.

## Part 9 — Marketing on ~$0

You cannot outspend anyone. You can out-*specific* them.

- **Instagram-first, reservation-driven scarcity.** A small room that's "hard to get into" beats a big empty one. Post the signature drink, the room, the ritual.
- **Regulars program:** remember names and orders (you're right there — that's the whole advantage of an owner-run bar). A handwritten "regulars" perk beats any ad.
- **Collaborate, don't compete:** guest bartender nights, local DJs, neighboring restaurants sending after-dinner traffic your way.
- **Own a night:** make one weeknight *yours* (a themed night) so there's a standing reason to come.

## Part 10 — De-risk before you sign anything: the 90-day validation

**Before committing to a lease and build-out, prove the demand cheaply.** This is the highest-leverage survival move given 60–70% failure odds.

1. **Run pop-ups / guest-bar nights** at existing venues with your concept and menu. Real customers, real prices, near-zero capital.
2. **Measure the one number that matters:** can you draw ~30–40 people who'll pay ~$16 and come back? Track repeat rate.
3. **Finalize the menu and pour costs** against real Caracas supplier prices during these tests.
4. **Only then** lock a location — and pick it to protect your break-even (off-prime rent, right zoning, confirmed *conformidad de uso* and permitted hours **before** signing **[VERIFIED gate]**).

If the pop-ups don't pull a crowd, you've learned it for a few hundred dollars instead of $40,000.

## The plan in one page

1. **Concept:** tiny (20–35 seats), cocktail-led speakeasy/neighborhood bar. Two people = the entire staff. Design to that ceiling.
2. **Validate first:** 90 days of pop-ups. Prove ~15+ paying guests/night is easy before you sign a lease.
3. **Structure lean:** C.A. for liability; stay an ordinary contributor to dodge IGTF-collection/SPE filing; get the alcohol-authorizing patente *before* the liquor license.
4. **Keep fixed costs tiny:** off-prime small local, no payroll, minimal kitchen. Break-even ≈ $3,700/mo ≈ 15 guests/night.
5. **Protect margin:** ≤25% pour cost, repriced monthly; USD pricing; spirits-as-inflation-hedge; formal invoiced suppliers only.
6. **Open with runway:** 3–6 months of fixed costs in USD, locked. Refill before drawing more.
7. **Govern the partnership in writing:** roles, money rules, exit terms, and pre-agreed tripwires to pivot or stop.
8. **Be resilient:** generator + fuel budget, disciplined cash/security, ~$0 marketing via regulars and scarcity.

**Bottom line:** you don't beat Caracas's bar-failure rate by being bigger or fancier. You beat it by having **the lowest break-even on the block, the highest margin per drink, a runway that outlasts a slow season, and a partnership that doesn't crack under stress.** Small, cheap, differentiated, and cash-disciplined is not the compromise — it *is* the winning strategy here.
